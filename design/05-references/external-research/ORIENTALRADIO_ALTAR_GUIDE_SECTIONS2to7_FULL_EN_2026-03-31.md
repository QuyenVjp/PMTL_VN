## SECTION 2 — INTRODUCTION OF BODHISATTVAS WORSHIPPED ON THE GUAN YIN CITTA ALTAR

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/2-introduction-of-bodhisattvas-worshipped-on-the-guan-yin-citta-altar/`

### 2.1 Guan Yin Bodhisattva (Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát)

**Introduction**

Guan Yin Bodhisattva is the symbol of compassion and wisdom in Buddhism. She plays an important part in Mahayana Buddhism and folkloric beliefs. The spirit of great compassion, as represented by Guan Yin Bodhisattva, is perceived as the foundation of Mahayana Buddhism.

According to Buddhist scriptures, Guan Yin Bodhisattva is the manifestation of the "Buddha of True Dharma Light". Across immeasurable lands, she takes the form of a Bodhisattva to seek the cries of sufferings and relieve sentient beings from distress. There is a special affinity between Guan Yin Bodhisattva and Amitabha Buddha. She is also one of the Three Saints of the Western Pure Land.

With great compassion, Guan Yin Bodhisattva made a vow to help all beings selflessly far and wide. If sentient beings wholeheartedly call for Guan Yin Bodhisattva whenever they encounter difficulties and sufferings, they will be saved by the Bodhisattva. Guan Yin Bodhisattva can manifest into various forms according to the needs of different sentient beings in order to teach the right Dharma.

Among the various Bodhisattvas in Buddhism, Guan Yin Bodhisattva is the most well-known and commonly worshipped. There is a saying: "Every family pays respects to Amitabha Buddha; every household prays to Guan Yin Bodhisattva." Mount Putuo in Zhoushan City, Zhejiang Province, China, has always been known as the Buddhist practice place of Guan Yin Bodhisattva.

**Pray to Guan Yin Bodhisattva when encountering difficulties**
*(Master Jun Hong Lu's Dharma Talk at the Guan Yin Temple)*

Guan Yin Bodhisattva, the most compassionate among the Buddhas. As a representation of a compassionate saviour, Guan Yin Bodhisattva alleviates those in pain with great mercy and compassion. When you are faced with difficulties, you should first pray to Guan Yin Bodhisattva, as she will always relieve sentient beings from pain and suffering.

During the time of Shakyamuni Buddha, He told the sentient beings to pray to Guan Yin Bodhisattva. Shakyamuni Buddha said, "Guan Yin Bodhisattva attained Buddhahood countless eons ago." She attained Buddhahood before Shakyamuni Buddha. She manifests the form of a Bodhisattva out of her great compassion and readiness to save sentient beings.

Among all Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, Guan Yin Bodhisattva is the symbol of compassion. Every Bodhisattva made different vows that led them to attaining Buddhahood:
- Guan Yin Bodhisattva embodies **great compassion**
- Manjushri Bodhisattva embodies **great wisdom**
- Samantabhadra Bodhisattva embodies **great practice**
- Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva embodies **great vows**

As all Bodhisattvas made different vows and are unique in their own ways, you should never make comparisons among Bodhisattvas.

**Three special days related to Guan Yin Bodhisattva**

A Buddhist practitioner can offer First Incense on these days. It is advisable to adopt a full vegetarian diet, recite scriptures, refrain from killing, perform life liberation and do more meritorious deeds on these days:

- **19th day of the 2nd lunar month** — Birthday of Guan Yin Bodhisattva
- **19th day of the 6th lunar month** — Enlightenment Day of Guan Yin Bodhisattva
- **19th day of the 9th lunar month** — Renunciation Day of Guan Yin Bodhisattva

**Guan Yin Bodhisattva's three Dharma instruments: Willow, Vase and Lotus Seat**
*(Master Jun Hong Lu's Discourse – 27 March 2016)*

- **Willow**: Symbolises the prosperity of Buddhism. Guan Yin Bodhisattva uses the willow to bless us with dew and relieve us from disasters and difficulties.
- **Vase**: Contains "glaze water" — she blesses by pouring the water on you rather than sprinkling a few drops.
- **Lotus Seat**: Lotus flowers remain pure and untainted despite growing in mud — they symbolise kindness, cleanliness, and purity.

### 2.3 Tai Sui Bodhisattva (Thái Tuế Bồ Tát)

**Tai Sui Bodhisattva is on duty on a rotational basis** *(Wenda20120217 04:23)*

Tai Sui Bodhisattva is the Bodhisattva of the year. He is a Dharma Protector managing and supervising kind and evil deeds in the human world. He is also a deity in Heaven.

**Why should we pray to Tai Sui Bodhisattva?** *(Wenda20160115 01:03:50)*

Not only should we pray to Tai Sui Bodhisattva whenever we have "conflicts with Tai Sui" for a particular year, but also in the times that we don't. He is the Bodhisattva on duty for the year. You should pray to him regardless.

**Tai Sui Bodhisattva supervises Heaven and the Underworld** *(Wenda20160205 01:26:46)*

Caller: What should we do to benefit Tai Sui Bodhisattva?
Master Lu: You can simply pay respect to him by offering incense and praying regularly. Tai Sui Bodhisattva isn't a corrupt official; he doesn't require you to make offerings to him.

When people have conflicts with Tai Sui, do you think wearing a pendant would resolve the conflict? You need to pay respect and pray to him.

**Having "conflicts with Tai Sui"** *(Wenda20160115 01:03:50)*

Caller: What does "conflicts with Tai Sui" mean?
Master Lu: Having "conflicts with Tai Sui" means that the predestined karmic obstacles will increase in severity and the punishments will also be harsher. It is like having a police car behind you — your slightest wrongdoing will be caught. In the end you will panic.

**Tai Sui Bodhisattva changes every year — use a paper tablet, not a specific image** *(Buddhism: Your Questions Answered Q29)*

Question: I am praying to the image of Tai Sui Bodhisattva on the altar. Should I change it every year?

If removing the previous specific image:
1. Recite Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance 21 times, Great Compassion Mantra 7 times, Heart Sutra 7 times
2. Say: "Thank you to Tai Sui Bodhisattva, for helping me, [full name], and my family in the past."
3. First prayer: offer water, fruit and oil. Recite holy name of Tai Sui Bodhisattva 108 times, then Xiao Zai Ji Xiang Shen Zhou 21 times.

If you pray to Guan Yin Bodhisattva, she will resolve the conflicts for you; but if you pray to the Dharma Protectors, they will expel the demon which might create karmic knots between you and the spirit. There was a woman who asked Guan Di Bodhisattva to punish her husband — her husband had a car accident and she ended up needing to take care of him at home.

He is the embodiment of righteousness and loyalty in Heaven and the Underworld. No evil thought can ever occupy his mind. He's very pure and upright. He will help sentient beings only based on the rules of Heaven.

**Qualities to learn from Guan Di Bodhisattva**: Compassion and righteousness. He always does the right thing. We call him an "exemplary man of righteousness".

## SECTION 4 — DESIGNING AND ARRANGING A BUDDHIST ALTAR

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/4-designing-and-arranging-a-buddhist-altar/`

### 4.1 Different styles of an altar

**1) An open altar** — Recommended style.

**2) An enclosed altar** — When conditions don't allow (messy environment, close to kitchen or couple's room), use an enclosed altar with a curtain or cabinet door. Open when making incense offerings.

**Different styles of platforms underneath the images/statues of Bodhisattvas** *(optional — wooden platform to raise height)*:
- Podium-style
- T-shaped
- Bar-shaped

**Types of cloth used on an altar**:
- Golden-coloured napped fabric
- Golden-coloured thick satin

The altar will look more dignified with a piece of cloth covering the sides of the table. Yellow or golden-yellow napped fabric/satin is best.

**Components of a sample Buddhist altar**:
- Incense burner
- Fruit tray
- Water cup
- Oil lamp
- Vase

**It's not appropriate to offer pearls on an altar** *(Wenda20131011 36:05)*: Inappropriate. Remove pearls. Recite Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance 17 times.

**The platform for the image/statue must be made of wood. Do not use stones** *(Wenda20140126B 14:56)*: Stone belongs to the element of Yin and cannot be used as the platform.

**Do not wear prayer beads made from ivory or place them on an altar** *(Wenda20111225 94:33)*: Do not wear them, and do not place them on the altar. Wrap them with a piece of red cloth instead.

**A Buddhist altar should only be used to make offerings to Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, not auspicious creatures** *(Wenda20141005A 1:01:35)*: Animal statues should not be on the altar.

**Crystals and Dharma instruments on an altar** *(Collection of Master Jun Hong Lu's discourses 3)*: Do not place Dharma instruments on the altar. Feng shui stones, fossils, and purple crystals are not advisable to be placed at home. Scriptures are very sacred and have an aura, whereas crystals are transparent which attract spirits easily.

---

### 4.2 The surroundings of an altar

**Inappropriate books placed near an altar will produce a negative impact** *(ZongShu20160128 35:16)*: The area underneath and next to the altar should be tidy. If there are inappropriate contents in the books nearby, this will have a negative impact.

**Can a vase be hung on the wall next to the altar?** *(Wenda20120219 01:04:00)*: It is okay for a vase to be hung on the wall next to the altar if there is not enough space on the altar.

### 4.3 The yellow landscape painting (tranh phong cảnh vàng)

- **Do not frame the landscape painting.** A blessed landscape painting has no boundary. Once framed, its energy will be limited.

- **Origin of the yellow landscape painting** *(Wenda20161216 51:08)*: This landscape is unique and contains enormous power from Guan Yin Bodhisattva. It embodies both Yin and Yang — Water is Yin and the mountain is Yang. Hence, this image harmonises Yin and Yang.

- **Do not place red cloth behind an altar** *(Wenda20141214A 02:06)*: No, otherwise Bodhisattva will not visit.

- **The yellow landscape painting can only be placed behind the altar** *(Wenda20130714A 02:33)*: The blue landscape painting can regulate feng shui; the yellow one is a sacred scene blessed by the Bodhisattvas.

- **Height of the yellow landscape painting** *(Wenda20120608 01:22:52)*: The mountain can be slightly higher than the image of the Bodhisattva. Try to have the Bodhisattva's back against the mountain and the feet on floating clouds (water).

- **Things to watch out for when sticking the yellow landscape painting** *(Wenda20130712 04:35)*: Do this only when the incense and the oil lamp are extinguished. Keep reciting the Heart Sutra during the whole process.

- **The yellow landscape painting should not be lacking water** *(Wenda20161127A 46:33)*: The mountain and water form Yin and Yang. If there's only a mountain, the Yang energy will be too strong.

- **When the landscape painting fades, replace it** *(Wenda20151023 33:06)*: Just replace it without special rituals.

---

### 4.4 The viewing angle, height and width of an altar

The statues or images of Buddhas and Bodhisattvas should be slightly above eye level. If they are too low, place something (such as a nice box) underneath to raise their height.

---

### 4.5 Lighting in the Buddhist hall

- **Avoid cool white lighting or chandelier above the image of Bodhisattvas** *(Wenda20130818B 59:18)*: Cool white light attracts spirits easily — called "Yin light". Use warm white instead — "Yang light". Do not place a chandelier above images or statues. Lighting should only come from the front.

- **The lamps on an altar can't be on 24 hours a day** *(Wenda20120629 56:10)*: The lights in the Buddhist hall can be on 24 hours, but not the lotus lamps on the altar.

- **The lighting around the altar should be brighter at night-time** *(Wenda20130526A 52:50)*.

- **The lotus lamps should be switched off when incense isn't burning** *(Wenda20140105A 28:27)*: Once the lotus lamps are on, you need to light up the incense. Once the incense is finished burning, the lamps should be switched off straightaway.

---

### 4.6 Altar cabinet details

- **Shelves under an altar**: Can store scripture books, Buddhist instruments and prayer mats. Do not place sundries or irrelevant books.

- **Material**: Rosewood is better, but do not use old wood. Old wood contains spiritual energy and carries the Yin energy. Use solid wood in a reddish colour. Do not use a dark colour.

- **Best colour**: Milky white is okay, but better to cover with yellow cloth. Vermilion or dark colours like brown or coffee colour are also okay.

- **An altar should not be composed of individual compartments**: Best to place Bodhisattvas' statues in one cabinet.

- **Can a chest of drawers be used as an altar?** *(Wenda20140615B 15:44)*: Yes, but be cautious what you store in them. Socks, undergarments cannot be placed there. Cover with yellow cloth.

- **What if there's a corner behind the altar?** *(Wenda20130524 37:53)*: The altar cabinet should not have a triangle-shaped design with angles at the back.

## SECTION 5 — THINGS TO PAY ATTENTION TO WHEN PAYING RESPECT TO BODHISATTVA

### 5.1 Purpose of blessing offerings

*(Source: part-1)*

To bless an item means to endow Bodhisattva's special energy in items such as Bodhisattva's images, statues and Dharma instruments. Before blessing, items have no spiritual energy; blessing allows the minds of practitioners to resonate with the Buddha's mind.

**Benefits of each type of offering**:
- **Flowers**: Improve one's appearance; those who look dignified must have had spiritual cultivation in their previous life.
- **Oil**: Beneficial for eyesight and gaining wisdom.
- **Water**: Eases and calms the mind; helps eliminate greed as water doesn't cost anything. Should be changed daily.
- **Fruit**: Helps wishes materialize sooner. "Offering fruit makes the wishes of a kind-hearted person come true sooner."

**Duration of blessing effects**:
- Blue landscape paintings: Effective approximately 5 years; energy diminishes over time.
- Yellow landscape paintings: Remain effective longer when placed behind Bodhisattva statues.
- Blessed Bodhisattva images: "They won't be effective after two or three years" if left unused.
- To extend effectiveness: place unused blessed items on altar within 6–12 months and recite: "May Guan Yin Bodhisattva have compassion and give blessings so that the Buddha's light shines upon these images."

---

### 5.2 About Bodhisattva's images and statues

*(Source: part-2)*

**Storage and placement of images not yet on an altar**:
Wrap with red cloth and place on a clean surface. Never place them on stools or chairs — only clean tables are appropriate.

**Framing standards**:
Frames can be wood, aluminum alloy, or polymer materials, but **avoid silver-colored frames**. Master Lu recommends practitioners frame images themselves rather than using professional mounting services, as workers may have "defiled hands" affecting the images.

**What to avoid**:
- Embroidered images (considered disrespectful)
- Using images as decoration without proper consecration
- Broken or damaged images on active altars

When images aren't being used as altar pieces, recite the "Triple Sevens" (7 repetitions each of the Great Compassion Mantra, Heart Sutra, and Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance).

**Unsuitable materials for statues**:
- Transparent materials: attract spirits easily
- Marble: carries excessive Yin energy
- Copper, iron, tin, silver, gold: possess Yin properties — "if you sense sharp coldness from touching certain materials, they most likely carry the Yin energy"

**Recommended material**: Porcelain statues are superior — they can hold stronger energy from Bodhisattvas and absorb positive spiritual force more effectively.

**Selection**: Choose statues with which you feel personally connected. If a particular image appears to smile at you, that indicates a suitable choice.

---

### 5.3 About water offerings

*(Source: part-3)*

**Water cup requirements**:
- Brand new cups; best to come with a lid
- Avoid glass cups: "Spirits are transparent. They cling on to transparent materials easily"
- Never use cups with Buddhist scriptures engraved on them
- White porcelain cups without Bodhisattva images are appropriate
- Don't use cups featuring animal figures
- 1 cup per Bodhisattva; cups are dedicated — not mixed with other purposes

**Water types acceptable**:
- Hot or cold previously boiled water
- Mineral water, purified water
- Any drinkable water without taste, color, or odor

**Not permitted**: Tap water (untreated), tea

**Great Compassion Water (Nước Đại Bi)**:
- Water offered to Guan Yin Bodhisattva is "Great Compassion Water" — blessed by the Bodhisattva
- Effectiveness lasts approximately two to three days
- Don't microwave it; warm by placing cup in hot water instead
- Never water plants with it
- Don't drink directly from offering cups; pour into separate containers

**Health uses**:
- Wiping forehead/temples relieves tension
- Provides energy and confidence
- Contains adaptive properties
- Never apply to genital areas; inform Bodhisattva before applying below the navel

### 5.4 About oil offerings

*(Source: part-4)*

**Key benefits**: "Making offerings of vegetable oil to Buddhas and Bodhisattvas will result in improved eyesight, hearing and wisdom."

**Recommended oils**: Olive oil, canola seed oil, corn oil, lotus oil.

**Avoid**: Sesame oil, peanut oil, aromatic oils, soybean oil (strong scents or burning properties).

**Proper practice**: Light oil lamps and refill daily with small amounts of oil. Light the lamp with a match or lighter — **never blow out flames with breath** (carries impurities); use the lamp lid.

**Post-offering use**: Oil that has been offered can be used for cooking vegetarian dishes, but should not be used for preparing non-vegetarian meals.

**Number of lamps**: One lamp (small altars); four lamps (one per Bodhisattva); up to six lamps if space permits.

**Extinguish lamps before incense burns out**: Leaving them lit after the incense finishes is disrespectful to invited Bodhisattvas.

### 5.5 Flower offerings

*(Source: part-5)*

**Types of flowers suitable for altar**:
Lily, Lotus Flower, Peony, Gladiolus (Sword Lily), Narcissus, Tulip, Chrysanthemum, Carnation, Orchid, Daffodil.

Also: Lucky bamboo is suitable.

**Flowers to avoid**: Roses (thorns), peach blossoms, Japanese morning glories, sunflowers, potted plants with soil.

**Color preference**: Yellow and white flowers are better.

**Quantity of flower stalks**: Even total number when combining both vases. Count stalks, not individual flowers.

**Vase specifications**:
- Two symmetrical vases on both sides of the altar
- Porcelain vases are best
- Avoid glass or crystal vases (transparent attracts spirits)
- Avoid vases with Chinese poems, scripture texts, or animal patterns
- Lotus flower patterns are acceptable
- White vases are appropriate for lucky bamboo
- Vases should look symmetrical in size

**Different flowers can be placed in one vase**: Mixing types is acceptable.

**Flower maintenance**:
- Replace flowers once every week; do not leave wilted flowers
- Remove lily pollen when it falls on the altar surface
- Take vases off the altar before pruning/trimming; do not prune on the altar
- When handling items on the altar, recite the Heart Sutra simultaneously

**Spiritual benefit**: "Flowers offered to the Bodhisattva will be imbued with divine energy. They will improve the energy field in our home."

### 5.6 Offerings of fruit (trái cây cúng)

*(Source: part-6)*

**Recommended fruits**: Apple, pomelo, orange, mandarin, mango, pineapple, watermelon, dragon fruit — preferably fragrant varieties.

**Fruits to avoid**: Bananas, peaches, pears, green-colored fruits, dried fruits or nuts, artificial fruit.

**Presentation rules**:
- Quantity per layer should be an **odd number** (e.g., three apples on bottom, one on top)
- Multiple plates can be odd or even
- Fruit must rest on a plate, never directly on altar
- Use **one fruit type per plate**; no mixing different types
- Offer **whole fruit**, not sliced pieces
- Fruit must be washed and labels removed before offering
- Remove fruit once decay begins; if fruit falls, it cannot be re-offered

**Consumption after offering**: Fruit blessed by Bodhisattvas may be eaten afterward. Blessed fruit helps devotees "take in divine energy and the light of Buddhas."

---

### 5.7 About incense

*(Source: part-7)*

**Type & material**:
- "Smoke-free and stick-free straight sandalwood incense is the best option"
- Avoid incense coils, aromatic varieties, and cones
- Each incense type invites different Bodhisattvas
- Sandalwood specifically offered to Guan Yin Bodhisattva
- Avoid sticks made from bamboo or wood
- Do not use broken sticks

**Physical handling**:
- Never point incense at Bodhisattvas; **hold vertically**
- If dividing long incense, use rounded-end scissors (not hand-snapped) and inform Bodhisattvas first
- Complete the full offering ritual when continuing incense, including seven prostrations before and after

**Consistency**: Avoid changing incense brands. When necessary, inform Bodhisattvas: "This is another type of incense I bought, please accept this."

**Incense burner material & design**:
- Porcelain burners preferred over metal (metal represents "Yin energy")
- Avoid burners with Heart Sutra inscriptions or animal imagery
- Bronze burners with fortune/longevity characters are acceptable
- Position directly in front of each Bodhisattva
- Better to place one incense burner in front of each Bodhisattva

**Incense burner placement**: Position directly in front of each Bodhisattva. Placing incense burner for each Bodhisattva shows greater respect.

**Ash management**: Use ash catchers to prevent falling residue. Fill new burners with millet or rice initially until ash accumulates naturally.

**Symbolic meanings of incense smoke patterns**:
- Curled incense patterns indicate Bodhisattva/Dharma Protector presence
- Loop formations with protruding ends suggest "things will be achieved regardless of all obstructions"
- Frequency of curling correlates with cultivation level

---

### 5.8 Notes on prayer mat (chiếu lạy)

*(Source: part-8)*

**Acceptable designs**: Lotus patterns. Text like "Lotus Prayer Mat" with decorative elements is acceptable. **Do not include Buddha names** (Guan Yin Bodhisattva, Amitabha, etc.) on mats.

**Acceptable colors**: Red and yellow.

**Colors to avoid**: Black and white — considered unsuitable.

**Proper usage etiquette**:
- Prayer mats should **never be sat upon** during use — the human body contains both yin (lower body) and yang (upper body) energies that should not be mixed on the same mat during prostrations

**Accidental mishandling**:
- If a mat is accidentally kicked or stepped over: recite Qi Fo Mie Zui Zhen Yan 7 times
- If someone sits on the mat unintentionally: recite Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance once
- Mats remain usable afterward

**Storage**: When not in use, place mats beneath the altar. If stored beside the altar, position them upright to prevent others from stepping over them.

## SECTION 6.1 — ETIQUETTE AND TABOOS ABOUT AN ALTAR

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/6-1-etiquette-and-taboos-about-an-altar/`

**Reverence matters the most** *(Wenda20161113A 48:30)*:

When you worship the Bodhisattvas at the prayer hall, you must:
- Dress properly
- Keep your voice down
- Wash your hands before paying respect
- Look at the Bodhisattvas first before offering incense

Do not offer incense and pay respect while someone is taking a shower nearby.

**Refrain from paying respect to Buddhas and Bodhisattvas**:
- Within **15 minutes** after using the toilet (refers to defecation)
- Within **five hours** after having intimate acts

**Dress code** *(Wenda20140704 19:35)*:
- You must not pay respects to Bodhisattvas with slippers on — take them off before the prayer mat
- Trousers must cover the knees (ladies must wear trousers)
- Underwear must be worn inside

**Look at the Bodhisattvas before worship** *(Wenda20131229A 03:05)*:
- Say the names of each Bodhisattva when paying respect
- You need to look at the Bodhisattvas and bow — you can't say it without looking
- Need to look at all three Bodhisattvas

**Notes on paying respects at the Guan Yin Citta Centre** *(Wenda20130614 18:39)*:
- Prayer mats must not be sat on
- Clean your hands before changing the water on the altar; do not touch the rim of the water cup
- Do not mix up different Bodhisattvas' cups
- When inserting incense, silently recite: "My gratitude to Guan Yin Bodhisattva. I, [name], am offering incense to Bodhisattvas. May the Bodhisattvas bless me."
- Do not speak loudly in the temple

**Questions regarding prostrations during menstruation** *(Shuohua 20121019 23:35)*:
Making prostrations during menstruation is not forbidden — you can keep yourself clean and perform a short prostration. A human being's body is defiled even without menstruation.

**Auspicious days for setting up an altar**:
- Choose: The 1st or 15th day of every lunar month; mornings of Bodhisattvas' birthdays
- Avoid: Gloomy, stormy or rainy days
- Best times: 8 am, 10 am

## SECTION 6.2 — THE PROCEDURE OF SETTING UP AN ALTAR

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/6-2-the-procedure-of-setting-up-an-altar/`

**Wash your hands before the ceremony.**

**Step 1: Put the offerings on the altar.** Using both hands, raise each Buddhist instrument slightly above your head before placing them.

**Order of Bodhisattvas' images/statues**:
1. ① Guan Yin Bodhisattva (center)
2. ② Nanjing Bodhisattva
3. ③ Tai Sui Bodhisattva
4. ④ Guan Di Bodhisattva (set of three: Guan Ping, Guan Di, Zhou Tsang)

Then: incense burners → oil lamp → water cups → fruit → flowers.

**Step 2: Light the oil lamp.** If you have an electrical lotus lamp, turn it on prior to lighting the oil lamp.

**Step 3: Light the incense.** Use the oil lamp to light up three incense sticks; in a vertical position, raise them above your eyebrows and bow three times. Place the three incense sticks into the incense burner together. **The tip of the incense should not point towards Bodhisattva.**

**Step 4: Light the "grand incense" (sandalwood stick) — three times.** Light the sandalwood stick using the oil lamp, extinguish the fire (do not blow it out with your mouth) — the smoke released is the "grand incense", the fragrance of Bodhisattva. Repeat three times.

**Step 5: Kneel and sincerely invite each Bodhisattva** (say each prayer three times):

- *"I, [full name], sincerely invite the Greatly Merciful and Greatly Compassionate Guan Yin Bodhisattva to enter the sacred image/statue of Bodhisattva on my altar."* → Recite Great Compassion Mantra 7 times and Heart Sutra 7 times.

- *"May the Greatly Merciful and Greatly Compassionate Guan Yin Bodhisattva invite Nanjing Bodhisattva to enter the sacred image/statue of the Bodhisattva on my altar."* → Recite holy name of Nanjing Bodhisattva 108 times.

- *"May the Greatly Merciful and Greatly Compassionate Guan Yin Bodhisattva invite Tai Sui Bodhisattva to enter the sacred statue of the Bodhisattva on my altar."* → Recite holy name of Tai Sui Bodhisattva 108 times; Xiao Zai Ji Xiang Shen Zhou 21 times.

- *"May the Greatly Merciful and Greatly Compassionate Guan Yin Bodhisattva invite Guan Di Bodhisattva, Zhou Tsang Bodhisattva and Guan Ping Bodhisattva to enter the sacred images/statues of the Bodhisattvas on my altar."* → Recite holy names of the respective Bodhisattvas 108 times.

*(Note: If the images/statues have been blessed by Master Lu, they may be put directly onto the altar and the above step can be omitted. Recite the Great Compassion Mantra and the Heart Sutra 7 times each after inviting.)*

**Step 6: Kneel and bow.** Generally make 1 or 3 prostrations to each Bodhisattva, or a total of 7 or 13 prostrations.

**Step 7: Make vows.** Assess your capabilities before making vows: refrain from killing, be vegetarian, offer morning and evening incense, share the Buddha-dharma, sponsor printing of scripture booklets, etc.

**Step 8: Make prayer requests.** Based on your circumstances: good health, smooth career, harmonious family, etc.

**Step 9: Express gratitude and perform full prostrations** (7 or 13):

> "My deepest gratitude to Namo the Greatly Merciful and Greatly Compassionate Guan Yin Bodhisattva;
> My deepest gratitude to Namo Nanjing Bodhisattva;
> My deepest gratitude to Namo Tai Sui Bodhisattva;
> My deepest gratitude to Namo Guan Di Bodhisattva, Namo Zhou Tsang Bodhisattva, Namo Guan Ping Bodhisattva;
> My deepest gratitude to all Buddhas, Bodhisattvas and Dharma Protectors."

**Take a step back from the prayer mat.**

## SECTION 6.3 — BASIC PROCEDURES OF PAYING RESPECT TO THE BODHISATTVAS ON A DAILY BASIS

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/6-3-basic-procedures-of-paying-respect-to-the-bodhisattvas-on-a-daily-basis/`

*Offer morning and evening incense on a daily basis.*

**Step 1: Change the water, fruit, and flowers.**
Change water before lighting the oil lamp for morning incense. Pour the Great Compassion Water and the sacred water into your own cups — do not touch the cups of Bodhisattvas with your lips. Fill with boiled water (cooled), or spring water from a bottle.

**Step 2: Turn on electric lotus lamps, light oil lamps and incense.**
- Morning incense: after 5 am — normally 6 am, 8 am, 10 am, or 12 pm
- Evening incense: 4 pm, 6 pm, 8 pm, or 10 pm — **last incense must not be offered after 10 pm**
- If only one incense burner: offer three sticks together, morning and evening
- If multiple burners: one stick per Bodhisattva, or three on special days (1st/15th lunar month, Bodhisattvas' birthdays)
- May offer Grand Incense on special days after regular incense offering

**Step 3: Perform kneeling prostrations.**
- 1 or 3 prostrations per Bodhisattva, or 7 or 13 total
- Order: Guan Yin Bodhisattva → Nanjing Bodhisattva → Tai Sui Bodhisattva → Guan Di Bodhisattva → Zhou Tsang Bodhisattva → Guan Ping Bodhisattva

**Step 4: Make vows, prayers, and recite sutras.**
After prostrations: make vows or prayers, recite the Great Compassion Mantra and Heart Sutra once, or Qi Fo Mie Zui Zhen Yan 7 times. Then perform 7 or 13 full prostrations, stand up, put palms together and step away.

**Step 5: Extinguish oil lamps and turn off electric lotus lamps.**
Extinguish the oil lamp **before** the incense finishes burning. Turn off the electric lotus lamps at the end.

## SECTION 7 — ALTAR-KEEPING

> Source: `https://orientalradio.com.sg/eng-altar-setup-guide/7-altar-keeping/`

### 7.1 Tending Buddhist instruments, offered fruit and fruit trays

**Fruit preparation**: Wash fruit and remove labels before offering. Replace the entire tray — not individual pieces. Fruit can only be changed after incense sticks finish burning. Frequency: weekly or more often. **The fruit taken from the altar must not be offered to ancestors.** Spoiled fruit must be replaced promptly — never leave degraded offerings on the altar.

**Fruit tray maintenance**: Sticky tape residue may be cleaned with detergent (avoid strong fragrances). Old trays should be disposed of, not given to other practitioners.

**Water cups**: Do not wash frequently. Occasional detergent use is permissible if hands remain clean and cups are thoroughly rinsed. Yellow stains that cannot be removed warrant replacement — wrap old cups carefully before disposal.

**Incense burners and ash**:
- Unburnt incense sticks: remove promptly using tweezers
- Ash falling on altar surfaces: sweep away with clean brushes
- When burners become overfull: scoop excess ash, wrap in paper, dispose
- Use ash catchers to prevent overflow
- Old incense burners: wrap in **red cloth** before disposal

**Flowers**: Vases require regular cleaning and water changes. Lily pollen and withered petals need timely removal. Trim flowers away from the altar before placement. **Empty vases cannot remain on the altar.**

### 7.2 Cleaning Bodhisattva images

- Use only smooth, clean cloth without water unless dry cloth proves insufficient
- Never use detergent or alcohol
- When water is necessary, use clean water only
- Wipe gently when incense is not burning

### 7.3 Broken Buddhist instruments and torn images

**Accidental breakage**: Breaking altar cups accidentally requires reciting Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance three times. Bodhisattvas forgive unintentional damage.

**Damaged images during delivery**: Scratches in non-critical areas (mountains, water backgrounds) are acceptable. Scratches directly on images require replacement. Damaged packaging: sender must recite Eighty-Eight Buddhas Great Repentance 7 times.

**Irreparably damaged images**: Wrap and store properly.

**Partially damaged blessed statues**: Recite repentance 108 times total. Statues with intact Bodhisattva bodies may be repaired and placed on altars.

### 7.4 Other considerations

- Used Buddhist instruments (incense burners, oil lamps, vases, fruit trays) may be given to other practitioners, **except**: the cups on the altar — these cannot be given to others.
- Altar drapes should be washed in dedicated basins.
- Blackened ceilings above altars from incense smoke are acceptable; painting is optional.
